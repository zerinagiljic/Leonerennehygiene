01 — SENSITIVITY

For sharp, fleeting pain

That jolt from cold drinks, hot coffee, or a spoonful of ice cream. The goal is to calm the nerve and seal off the exposed surface.

Toothpaste

Desensitizing toothpaste

Potassium nitrate or stannous fluoride

The active ingredient quiets the nerve inside the tooth and plugs the microscopic channels that carry cold and sweet straight to it. Use it twice daily — relief builds over about two weeks rather than instantly.

Key
5% potassium nitrate
Best for
Cold & sweet twinges
Try
Sensodyne Pronamel, Crest Pro-Health
Toothbrush

Extra-soft toothbrush

Soft or extra-soft bristles only

Hard bristles and heavy scrubbing wear down enamel and push the gums back, exposing the sensitive root underneath. Soft bristles clean just as well without the damage that started the sensitivity.

Look for
Soft / extra-soft
Best for
Heavy-handed brushers
Try
Oral-B Sensitive Clean, Colgate Extra Clean Soft
02 — CAVITY PREVENTION

To strengthen and protect

Stopping decay before it ever needs a filling. These rebuild weak spots in enamel and starve the bacteria that cause cavities.

Toothpaste

Fluoride toothpaste

1,450 ppm fluoride

Fluoride re-hardens the early soft spots in enamel and makes the surface far more resistant to acid attacks. It's the single most proven daily defense against cavities — the one non-negotiable on this list.

Key
1,450 ppm fluoride
Best for
Everyone, daily
Try
Colgate Total, Crest Cavity Protection
Rinse

Fluoride mouth rinse

Alcohol-free, daily anti-cavity

A swish delivers an extra layer of fluoride to the grooves and gaps a brush skips over. Especially worth it if you're cavity-prone or have crowns, bridges, and other dental work to protect.

Look for
0.05% sodium fluoride
Best for
Cavity-prone mouths
Try
ACT Anticavity Fluoride Rinse
Mints / Gum

Xylitol mints or gum

100% xylitol-sweetened

Cavity bacteria can't feed on xylitol the way they do regular sugar, so their numbers drop — and chewing stimulates the saliva that naturally rinses teeth clean. A smart move after meals when you can't brush.

Key
Xylitol first ingredient
Best for
After meals, on the go
Try
Trident or Spry Xylitol Gum
03 — WHITENING

To brighten, safely

Lifting stains without wrecking enamel. There's a right tool for surface stains and another for deeper ones — knowing the difference saves your teeth.

Toothpaste

Whitening toothpaste

Low-abrasivity (RDA) formula

Gentle polishing agents lift the everyday film from coffee, tea, and red wine. The key is choosing a low-abrasivity formula that brightens the surface without scratching the enamel you're trying to show off.

Look for
RDA under 150
Best for
Coffee & tea stains
Try
Colgate Optic White Advanced
Strips

Peroxide whitening strips

Enamel-safe hydrogen peroxide

A measured dose of peroxide breaks apart the deeper stains set inside the tooth for a genuinely brighter shade. Follow the schedule exactly — and check with us first if you have sensitivity or lots of dental work.

Key
Hydrogen peroxide
Best for
Deeper, set-in stains
Try
Crest 3D Whitestrips Pro Effects
04 — GUM HEALTH

To calm inflammation

If your gums bleed when you brush, that's early gum disease — and it's reversible. These target the bacteria along and just below the gumline.

Toothpaste

Stannous fluoride toothpaste

Antibacterial + anti-cavity

Stannous fluoride is antibacterial — it cuts down the plaque bacteria that inflame gums and make them bleed, while still guarding against cavities. It's the gum-health upgrade over a plain fluoride paste.

Key
Stannous fluoride
Best for
Bleeding, puffy gums
Try
Crest Gum Detoxify, Parodontax
Device

Water flosser

Pulsed water irrigation

A pulsing jet of water flushes plaque and trapped food from right along the gumline and between teeth. It's a lifesaver around braces, bridges, and implants — and for anyone who finds string floss fiddly.

Use
Once daily, warm water
Best for
Braces, bridges, implants
Try
Waterpik Aquarius
Rinse

Antiseptic mouth rinse

Alcohol-free, anti-gingivitis

An antigingivitis rinse lowers the bacterial load across your whole mouth to settle and prevent gum inflammation. Pick an alcohol-free formula so it doesn't leave your mouth dry and irritated.

Look for
"Antigingivitis"
Best for
Whole-mouth gum care
Try
Listerine Antiseptic Original
05 — GUM RECESSION

To protect exposed roots

When gums pull back, the softer root surface is left bare — more sensitive and more prone to decay. The aim here is gentle, thorough cleaning.

Toothbrush

Extra-soft toothbrush

Gentle technique, no scrubbing

Once gums have receded, how you brush matters more than ever. An extra-soft brush cleans the exposed root surface thoroughly without driving the recession any further back.

Look for
Extra-soft bristles
Best for
Receding gumlines
Try
Philips Sonicare Sensitive Head, Oral-B Sensitive Gum Care
Toothpaste

Sensitivity toothpaste

For exposed root surfaces

Bared roots feel cold and sweet sharply and soften faster than enamel. A combined sensitivity-and-fluoride paste both soothes the nerve and helps shield the vulnerable root from decay.

Key
Fluoride + desensitizer
Best for
Sensitive exposed roots
Try
Sensodyne Nourish Healthy Gums
Interdental

Interdental brushes

Sized to the gap

Recession opens small triangular spaces between teeth that floss can skim right past. A correctly-sized interdental brush slips in and cleans those gaps gently and far more completely.

Tip
We'll size them for you
Best for
Gaps between teeth
Try
TePe Interdental Brushes, GUM Soft-Picks
06 — FRESH BREATH & DAILY CARE

The everyday basics

Unglamorous, but they do more heavy lifting than anything else on this page. Get these two habits down and most problems never start.

Floss

Floss or floss picks

Whichever you'll actually use

Brushing reaches only about three of every five tooth surfaces — flossing cleans the in-between spots where cavities and gum disease quietly begin. The best floss is honestly the one you'll reach for nightly.

Use
Once daily, any kind
Best for
Between every tooth
Try
Oral-B Glide, GUM Soft-Picks
Tool

Tongue scraper

Stainless steel, once daily

Most bad-breath odor comes from the bacterial coating on the back of the tongue. A scraper clears far more of it than brushing the tongue does — the fastest fix for morning breath there is.

Use
Front-to-back, A.M.
Best for
Fresher breath
Try
Dr. Tung's Stainless Steel Scraper
